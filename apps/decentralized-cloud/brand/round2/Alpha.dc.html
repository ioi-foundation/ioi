The construction. Four discs on a common base, each a flat token, and every region where two of them overlap drawn as its own token rather than as transparency. The cloud is not a shape anyone drew — it is only what the overlaps make. The smallest disc is the period from the wordmark, 16 units across on a 96 grid, which at a 24px icon renders 4px: the same 4px the 22px wordmark sets beside it. The period completes the cloud.
Against the category. iCloud is a white silhouette on blue, Cloudflare a flat orange silhouette, the generic hosting cloud an outline. All three are one shape drawn from the outside. This has no outline and no silhouette to draw — take one disc away and there is no cloud left, which is the point being made about venues.
The test this has to pass, and my own reading of it. The concept fails on its own terms if the discs do not read as a cloud without an outline. Drawn at 150px I think they do, just. At 24px and 16px above I do not think they do: the overlaps stop resolving, the four discs merge into one lobed mass, and what is left reads as bubbles or a blob rather than as anything meteorological. Judge it there rather than here.
The cost, stated. Overlapping circles are how clouds are drawn in almost every icon set, so the construction meant to be distinctive is also the most common way to build this exact shape. Two colourways are required, because one set cannot hold on both grounds. The one-ink reduction exists but inverts the meaning of the overlaps. And the mark carries seven fills where the product's whole argument is that a price should carry three fields.
