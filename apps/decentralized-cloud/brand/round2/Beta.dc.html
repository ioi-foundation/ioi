The construction. The name's own first letter, cut from the brand face, with the drawn period seated in its counter. Measured off a rendered specimen: the counter is 0.324 of the cap wide, the period is 0.250 of the cap, so it seats with 0.037 cap of clearance on each side. The face made room for it — the mark is not composed, it is found.
Against the category. Dropbox's early glyph, Discord's rounded shapes and the general letter-in-a-tile app icon all set a letter and stop. This one is a letter with a hole punched in it, and the hole is the other half of the wordmark — the same period that ends the name, in the only place the letter has room for it. Take the period out and it is just a "d"; that is the test it has to keep passing.
At size. It has structure at 16px — a stem, a bowl and a counter — where a bare disc has none, and it cannot be read as an indicator light because a light is not letter-shaped. One ink, knocked out, reversed: three reductions, one drawing.
The cost, stated. A single letter in a rounded tile is the most common app-icon form there is, and this one is doing nothing structurally that a hundred others do not. Its distinctiveness rests entirely on the face being unusual and on the counter being narrow enough to make the seated period look deliberate; if a reader does not register the period as the wordmark's period, all that is left is a lowercase d. And at 16px the counter is roughly one device pixel of clearance on each side of the hole, which is the edge of what will hold.
