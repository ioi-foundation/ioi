The construction. The name's own first letter, cut from the brand face, with the drawn period placed inside its counter. Measured off the rendered glyph: the counter is 1.064 of the cap wide and 0.600 tall, the period is 0.250 of the cap, so it sits with 0.407 cap of clearance at the sides, and 0.173 above and 0.179 below — which is to say it is centred on the TILE, at y 48, while the counter's own centre is y 48.15. Centred on the tile, not on the counter.

An earlier draft of this board printed 0.324 for that counter width and called the period tightly seated. That number was measured off a rasterised specimen on a page where nothing used the face, so the browser had silently substituted a default sans and the figure described a different letter entirely. It is corrected here, and the measurer now refuses to print anything unless the face has actually loaded.

The letter is a family monogram across the decentralized.* products — one construction, three derivations, the TLD cue as the differentiator — so which d it is cut from is settled separately. This specimen uses IOI Display's d. Judge the approach: a letterform with the wordmark's period seated in it.
Against the category. Dropbox's early glyph, Discord's rounded shapes and the general letter-in-a-tile app icon all set a letter and stop. This one is a letter with a hole punched in it, and the hole is the other half of the wordmark — the same period that ends the name, in the only place the letter has room for it. Take the period out and it is just a "d"; that is the test it has to keep passing.
At size, measured. Rendered at true device pixels and scanned down the centre of the tile, the period is a separate run of ink from the letter at 16px, 24px and 32px, with a gap of 1px, 2px and 4px above it. An earlier placement sat the period 8 units lower, and at 16px and 24px it welded to the bottom bar and stopped being a dot — the same scan reports two runs instead of three there, which is how the defect was found and how the fix is checked. One ink, knocked out, reversed: three reductions, one drawing.
The cost, stated. A single letter in a rounded tile is the most common app-icon form there is, and this one is doing nothing structurally that a hundred others do not. Its distinctiveness rests entirely on the face being unusual; if a reader does not register the period as the wordmark's period, all that is left is a D in a box, and at 24px and below the silhouette is close to a battery indicator. The honest word is placed, not seated: the counter is four times the period's width, so the dot sits in a large opening rather than being gripped by one, and nothing in the letterform makes its position inevitable. It is centred there because centring is defensible, not because the face demanded it.

One more, stated before anyone asks: because the letter is a family monogram, any gradient construction of it fails two rules this system already holds — every fill a token, and a reduction that is the same drawing in one ink. A gradient has neither. A flat-token form and a one-colour form would have to be defined first, with the gradient as the derived variant rather than the source.
