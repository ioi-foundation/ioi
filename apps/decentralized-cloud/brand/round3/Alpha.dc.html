The construction, read from the source. Three offset blocks with rounded corners, forming a d from stepped counters rather than from a bowl. Measured off the owner's vector: the visible mark is 108.63 × 96 units at aspect 1.132, and the corner radii cluster on two values, roughly 5.1 and 16.3 units, so the construction uses a small corner and a large one rather than one radius. Every path above is the source's own, translated and scaled, never redrawn.
Colour, and what flattening costs. The source carries three separate gradients on a common −34.8° axis, not one gradient across the mark. So a flat form cannot flatten "the" gradient: each block takes the midpoint of its own ramp. Two tokens result, not three — the second and third midpoints are #4078fa and #407dfa, five units apart on one channel out of 255 and indistinguishable — so the three-step sweep flattens to two steps, and the board says two rather than claiming three.
Against the category. Stepped offset blocks in a blue-to-cyan gradient is the house style of a decade of crypto and fintech marks, and this one does not escape it: the gradient, the slant and the rounded blocks are the genre's three signatures at once. What it has that the genre usually lacks is a real letter underneath — the steps do resolve into a d — and a construction with enough internal structure to derive from.
The family hinge is missing. The one thing the decentralized.* name owns is a period its brand face cannot set. This mark has no period in it. It appears in the lockups beside the wordmark, drawn as it always is, but it is not part of the mark — so the family's own distinguishing character sits outside the family's device. That is the sharpest thing that can be said against this construction and it is said here rather than left for a reviewer to find.
