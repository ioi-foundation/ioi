The base — the letter and the period after it — is byte-identical across the three. Only the tick inside the counter moves, and its position is the cue: left, centre, right for cloud, exchange and trade. Exchange and trade are proposals here; cloud is the one being decided.

The cue, measured — and a correction. Each sibling is differenced against the same mark drawn without its tick, so only the tick is measured: the three positions sit 4.71px apart at 32px, 3.54px at 24px and 2.32px at 16px, against a floor of one device pixel. Distinguishable at every size, 16px included.

An earlier draft of this board printed 0.54px at 16px and concluded the cue could not survive there. That was wrong by a factor of 2.6. It measured the tick's centre of mass across the counter's columns, which also contain the letter's inner edges, and those dominate the average and pull the three siblings together. The closed form was in the source the whole time — the tick steps 14.21 units, so separation is 14.21 × size ÷ 96 = 2.37, 3.55 and 4.74 — and the measurement now has to agree with that arithmetic or the run fails. What made 16px hard was never the spacing: it was the tick's width and the glyph's size in its tile, both drawing choices, and both changed.
One colour · stamp · dark ground
One ink on paper, knocked out of a tile, and reversed on a dark ground with no tile at all. The geometry is the same drawing in all three — same font-size, same baseline, same period, same tick — and the cue survives every one of them, which is why it is drawn rather than coloured. Where the letter is ink the period and tick are ink; where the letter is the hole they take the ground. In the two-colour lockups above, the period takes the brand green instead: a deliberate exception, stated, not the rule.

The construction, measured off the render. The name's own first letter, cut from the brand face, with the drawn period after it on the baseline — because that is where a period lives, and because this face closes the d with a bottom bar, so the counter's floor sits well above the baseline and a period inside it could never be on one. The period is cut to the stroke weight: 9.80 units against a measured stroke of 9.80, on a 96-unit grid, at a baseline of y 72.85. The tick is cut to the same stroke, which an earlier draft did not do. Side bearing letter-to-period is the wordmark's own rule, one ninth of the cap — 5.52 units — which is a different measure from the 3.99-unit tile margin, and an earlier draft of this board confused the two.

Fill. The cap is 51.8% of the tile's height and the mark 91.7% of its width. The horizontal binds: a letter-plus-period lockup is about 1.77:1 and the tile is square, so this is the largest the glyph goes without eating its margin. An earlier draft sat at 45%, and that one number caused the faint period, the sub-pixel tick and a bottom bar that dropped out at 16px.
Against the category. This is the letter-in-a-rounded-tile app icon, which is the most occupied form in software, and nothing about the tile or the letter escapes that. What is not in the category is the period: a second mark on the baseline, cut to the letter's own weight, which is the character the brand face cannot set and the only letterform this family owns outright.
Separation, at true device pixels. Scanning the baseline row: two runs at every size, with gaps of 2px at 16, 4px at 24 and 5px at 32. No optical correction is needed — the nominal bearing of one ninth of the cap, 5.52 units, holds throughout. At 16px the period renders at 98% of the letter's own coverage, because sizing the glyph to fill its tile took the period from 1.4 device pixels to 1.63. An earlier draft of this board, drawn at 45% tile fill, printed 49% and compared it against a letter it wrongly called 100%.
The cost, stated. The period is medial in "decentralized.cloud" — it separates, it does not end — so a mark that shows a letter and a following dot is showing the seam of the name rather than its close. Read quickly the tile is still a D with a speck. And the family's own arithmetic is unforgiving: all three siblings begin with the same letter, so everything distinguishing them rests on a 9.80-unit tick, which is why its measurement above is the number that matters most on this board.

The path not taken here. An independent review's route to a better mark is to replace the positional tick with a cue that is a SHAPE — one that distinguishes without needing a sibling beside it for reference, survives the knockout on its own terms, and carries some meaning about the product rather than being an index position. That is a redraw, not a correction, and it is deliberately not done here: this construction is being judged against another one, and redrawing it now would settle that comparison by effort rather than by merit. It is the stated next move if this construction is carried.
