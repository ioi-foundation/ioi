Each swatch names the token it is, in packages/design-system/tokens/colors.css. These nine carry meaning; four more are drawn and carry none — #ffffff paper, #ececec the softer hairline inside tables and rails, #1f1f1f body ink on the type specimens, and #3e7f5c the link hover. Thirteen in total, every one of them in that file, and none invented here. Exactly one was added to that file for this product rather than kept on a sheet: green-on-dark, because link green measures 3.53:1 on onyx and fails AA where this measures 8.78:1. A second was drafted and then deleted — a darkened red for the expired chip — once measurement showed red-600 already clears at 4.87:1 and the token was solving nothing. Every figure in this paragraph comes from brand/measure-contrast.mjs, which prints them from the hex values themselves; the first draft of this sheet asserted two contrast numbers from memory and both were wrong.

Statements the surface makes about itself
read-only surface last sweep shown while refreshing not wired — job api not live
One rule decides which kind a chip is. A chip reporting what the daemon said carries the daemon's token verbatim, in mono — nine of them, above, and a surface may not gloss one into friendlier English, because the reader who needs the chip is the reader who will search for that string. A chip reporting what THIS SURFACE is doing is a sentence in plain words, and there are exactly three. Nothing else is a chip.
Green means live, and only live. A candidate wears it when the daemon returned it as evidence_mode: live_evidence, and a source wears it when the daemon reports it answering — live_quote_source, available, storage_backends_engaged. It is never chrome: not the selection colour, not a callout accent, not the current tab. The one green that is not a status is the wordmark's own dot, which is the mark itself.
Grey is absence, red is time. A source with no adapter or no credential is grey and says why, in the daemon's own words, in the row where a price would have been. Red means one thing only: a quote whose expires_at has passed and which the surface has not yet refreshed. A state dot carries meaning, so it is drawn in grey-700 at 3.90:1 rather than a hairline grey that would fail the 3:1 floor for non-text.
Unwired is visible. Write surfaces designed ahead of the kernel carry the last chip and cannot be submitted. A stub a reader cannot tell apart from truth is refused; a labelled one is a design deliverable.
Freshness dial — a component, not a logo

3 min left
expired
The binding, written where the component is. The drawn arc is the window still good — its sweep is the fraction remaining, 1 − (now − observed_at) / (expires_at − observed_at), — and the grey it sits on is the window already spent. Both come from the candidate the daemon returned, never from a timer this surface started. Below 40px the dial reads as present-or-expired only and the text beside it carries the number; it does not claim a precision it cannot draw. At expires_at the arc is gone, the centre turns red, and the row stops being a price.
It is allowed to look like a progress ring, because that is what it is. This is the one place in the system where a ring is honest: it reports a real number, it changes as that number changes, and it never appears where there is no quote behind it. The identity does not use it — the wordmark's period is the only drawn thing in the logo, and a logo that appeared to report a live number would be the one thing this product cannot ship.
The centre disc is the same period the wordmark draws, at the same quarter-cap proportion. That is the whole family tie: the mark is punctuation, the dial is punctuation with a measure around it.

The endorsement sets IOI in the same face at 13px against the lockup's 28px, on one line, outside the clear-space boundary. Brand is not owner: decentralized.cloud proposes candidates, wallet.network authorizes, Hypervisor places and executes, Agentgres records what ran and what it cost.
