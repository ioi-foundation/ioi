Icon — the dot and its window
off the tile, on paper
The dot is the price and it is the same drawn period the wordmark uses — one object, one token on any given ground. The ring is the window that price is good for; the slice out of it is the part already spent.

The ring is cut by a straight vertical, not by a sweep. Both terminals are flat and parallel on one axis, which is what a measure with a piece taken out looks like and what a rotating spinner never does. Geometry is fixed at every size: dot 9, ring 26, cut at x=32 for a 104° gap, corner 11 on a 96-unit grid; only the stroke changes, 6 units at 32px and above and 9 below, with the dot opening to 11 to hold its counter. 16px is the floor. Below it the mark is the period alone.

The mark is a symbol, never an instance. Its gap is canonical and fixed; it does not report a real quote and is not bound to data. The product's window timeline is the instance — it quotes this geometry and binds its gap to an actual observed_at and expires_at. A logo that claimed a live number would be the one thing this product cannot ship.

The dot
One quarter of the cap height, rounded to the nearest pixel: 9px at 52 and 7px at 40 on this sheet, 6px at 34 on the system sheet, 5px at 28 on the direction boards, 4px at 22 in both product bars. Cap height measures 0.70 em in this face — 36.4px at 52 — taken off the rendered glyph, not the metrics table. The dot and the mark are one object and take one token on any ground: link-green on paper, green-on-dark on onyx, the same swap the ink makes.
Clear space
One cap height on every side, measured from the outermost ink — 36px around the 52px lockup above, and proportionally at every other size. Nothing enters it, the IOI endorsement line included.
Minimum size
Single line to 400px wide, which puts the cap at 14.6px. Narrow lockup to 220px, cap 11px. Below 220px the mark alone, down to a 16px floor. Never set the wordmark in another face and never track it past 0.01em.
