The identity is the name, and the only drawn thing in it is the period. IOI Display carries 33 glyph slots, 26 of them letters, and no full stop at all, so every character here is typed except one — and that one has to be made. A product whose name the brand face cannot finish is handed its own device by the gap.
There is no pictorial mark, deliberately. An earlier round drew one, a ring around the dot meaning the window a price is good for, and an independent review found it read as a loading spinner at 24px and as a stray letterform at 16px. It was not wrong about the product; it was wrong as a logo. It now lives where it is true — as the freshness dial beside a quote — and the identity keeps the part nobody can copy: this name, this face, this missing glyph.

App icon — the period in its tile
off the tile, on paper
Drawn to the face, not to a grid. IOI Display's stem measures 0.140 em off a rendered specimen. A period at one quarter of the cap height is 0.174 em, which is 1.24 stems — heavier than the stroke it punctuates, which is what a full stop has to be to hold at text size.

The tile is defined once and used everywhere: a 96-unit square, corner radius 11, the disc at radius 13 centred on x 48 and seated at y 62. That seat is the point — low, where a period sits on a baseline, not centred where a status light sits. Punctuation, never a control.

It survives 16px for the same reason a period survives 8pt type: a filled disc has no counter to lose. It reduces to one colour, prints, engraves and stamps, and there is no optical-size system because nothing here needs one.

Family, recorded 2026-09-04. The letter "d" is the monogram for the decentralized.* products as a family — one construction, three derivations across cloud, exchange and trade, with the TLD cue as the differentiator. No board here may claim a product-exclusive d. Which construction the family d is cut from is an open question settled elsewhere; this sheet governs only how cloud derives from it, which is the seated period.

The honest limit. A disc alone is not distinctive, and this sheet does not claim it is. Recognition is carried by the name, set in a face almost nobody else is using. The icon's only jobs are to be unmistakably the same object as the period in that name, and to never be mistaken for a control.

The period
Diameter is one quarter of the cap height, rounded to the nearest pixel: 9px at 52 and 7px at 40 here, 7px at 40 and 5px at 28 on the system sheet, 5px at 28 on the direction boards, 4px at 22 in both product bars. Side bearings are one ninth of the cap, which is 4px at 52, 3px at 40 and 2px at 28 and below. Every figure on this sheet comes from brand/measure-face.mjs, which rasterises a specimen and reads the ink.
Cap height
The font declares sCapHeight 700 of 1000. A rendered specimen inks 139px at 200px, so the measured cap is 0.695 em and the declared one is 0.700. This sheet uses the measured figure and states both, because they are not the same number.
Clear space and minimum size
One cap height clear on every side — 36px around the 52px lockup, 15.3px around a 22px lockup in a product bar. "decentralized" sets 13.18 em and "cloud" 5.61. The single line holds to 400px, cap 14.5px; the narrow lockup to 220px, cap 11.6px; a cap of exactly 11px would need 209px. Below 220px, the period alone.
