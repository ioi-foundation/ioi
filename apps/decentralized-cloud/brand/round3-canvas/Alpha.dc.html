Derivation proposal — owner-reversible. The base is the owner's geometry, byte-identical in all three: the paths are read out of the source vector and translated onto a 96-unit grid, never redrawn. The cue is a notch knocked out of ONE block, and which block is notched names the sibling — the construction's own three-part structure doing the work rather than a device imported from elsewhere. It is a knockout — an SVG mask, absent ink, not an opaque chip the colour of the paper — so one drawing serves paper, tile and dark ground alike and colour carries none of it. An earlier cut of this board drew the notch as a white rect and called it a knockout; on any non-white ground that is a second ink and needs the notch hand-matched to each ground.

The cost, stated. A notch at 16px is 13 units of a 96 grid — 2.1 device pixels — and it is competing with a mark that already has three internal edges of its own, so the notch reads as a nick in a corner rather than as a signal. And the reserved mark has no period: the family hinge, the one character the brand face cannot set, is absent from this construction and has to be added beside it in the lockup rather than being part of the mark.
One colour · dark ground
One ink and reversed on dark. The three blocks lose their separation entirely in one colour — the gradient is what tells them apart in the full form, and without it the mark becomes a single silhouette with a notch. The board does not claim otherwise: this is the reduction, and it is a different object from the coloured mark rather than the same drawing in fewer inks.

The construction, read from the source. Three offset blocks with rounded corners, forming a d from stepped counters rather than from a bowl. Measured off the owner's vector: the visible mark is 107.89 × 96 units at aspect 1.124, measured on the curves with getBBox rather than by scanning the path data — an earlier cut of this board said 108.63 at aspect 1.132, which is the control-polygon hull: 108.63 is literally a bezier control point in the first shape and lies outside the curve it steers. The construction's own corner radii cluster on two values, roughly 5.1 and 16.3 units, so the construction uses a small corner and a large one rather than one radius. Every path above is the source's own, translated and scaled, never redrawn.

It is not optically centred in its tile, and that is a defect rather than a decision: at the placement above the mark carries 20.5 units of air above it and 6.8 below, sitting low by three to one, while its left and right edges run close to the tile. The tile is also white on white paper here, so there is effectively no tile to judge it against. Both are fixable by moving the mark, not by redrawing it — but neither is fixed on this board, and a construction is judged as placed.
Colour, and what flattening costs. The source carries three separate gradients on a common −34.75° axis — userSpaceOnUse, three conformal rotation matrices of the same angle, and the gradients above are drawn that way rather than on each shape's own bounding-box diagonal, which would put them at 42.5°, 42.8° and 20.6°. Not one gradient across the mark. So a flat form cannot flatten "the" gradient: each block takes the midpoint of its own ramp. Two tokens result, not three — the second and third midpoints are #4078fa and #407dfa, five units apart on one channel out of 255 and indistinguishable — so the three-step sweep flattens to two steps, and the board says two rather than claiming three.
Against the category. Stepped offset blocks in a blue-to-cyan gradient is the house style of a decade of crypto and fintech marks, and this one does not escape it: the gradient, the slant and the rounded blocks are the genre's three signatures at once. What it has that the genre usually lacks is a real letter underneath — the steps do resolve into a d — and a construction with enough internal structure to derive from.
The family hinge is missing. The one thing the decentralized.* name owns is a period its brand face cannot set. This mark has no period in it. It appears in the lockups beside the wordmark, drawn as it always is, but it is not part of the mark — so the family's own distinguishing character sits outside the family's device. That is the sharpest thing that can be said against this construction and it is said here rather than left for a reviewer to find.
