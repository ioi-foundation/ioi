# Visual direction round one

Three directions, three screens each, judged by fresh cold readers against the reference
set ioi-c0 named: linear.app, vercel.com, stripe.com, railway.com, fly.io, and the
Hypervisor product UI's own shell for family.

The question, weighted 30 of 100 as the new ninth criterion: **would a stranger believe
this is a first-tier product in the first five seconds?**

All three carry the same identity — the head-to-head winning mark (B, dissolving cloud,
51/100) and the wordmark set entirely in IOI.ttf per the owner's ruling — so the
directions are judged on their systems and not on three different brands.

## The three

| | Axis | System | Stated tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Ledger** | Evidence as the aesthetic | Archivo + IBM Plex Mono, hairline rules, green only for live evidence | Quiet. Reads as serious, could read as plain. |
| **B · Instrument** | Inverted and instrumented | Space Grotesk + JetBrains Mono on onyx, green lifted to the on-dark token | Dark flatters screenshots and punishes long reading — and this product depends on honesty copy people read. |

## Reader one

**C 62 · A 54 · B 41. None reach the reference set.**

> "You have one direction (C) with a genuine idea and no craft budget spent on it yet,
> and two directions that are stylesheets. Take C, and spend the next pass on the things
> that aren't type."

On what decided it, for C: "the italic green *twelve minutes ago* set inside the serif
headline. That's an actual typographic decision — a second voice inside one sentence,
colour used once, for meaning. Nothing in A or B is a decision of that kind."

On A: "It's clean, but it's the default… Nothing in the frame was decided by a designer;
it was assembled by a good engineer with taste."

On B: "Dark navy plus mint-green mono numerals plus a rounded geometric sans is the
default costume of every crypto/DePIN dashboard of the last five years… When the accent
is on eight things it's on nothing."

### The locked step — the clearest win in the set

A and B render the locked Execute as a disabled BUTTON, and the reader called both
broken: "A disabled button is the universal signal of 'you did something wrong / this
isn't ready.' Only the paragraph underneath rescues it, and nobody reads the paragraph
in five seconds."

C made it an editorial column with the eyebrow **NOT OFFERED HERE** and no control at
all: "There is nothing to click, so there is nothing broken. 'NOT OFFERED HERE' as a
label is a positioning statement, not an error state. That's the difference between
designing the constraint and disabling a widget."

That inverts my own assumption. I built the locked control BECAUSE a boundary a reader
has to read to is a boundary they can miss — and a real disabled control reads as a
fault, which is worse than the paragraph it replaced.

### Tables

C: "the 2px black rule under the header row against hairline row separators — one strong
horizontal, everything else quiet — plus no card wrapper at all, so the table is the page
instead of an object floating on it."

A: "literally the Excel/Google Sheets visual… five identical badges stacked down the left
edge, all saying the same thing. If every row is live, the badge belongs in the header,
not the rows."

### What ALL THREE lack, in the reader's damage order

1. **Any visual asset whatsoever.** "Nine screenshots and there is not one image,
   illustration, diagram, gradient, texture, or piece of motion. Every reference on your
   list carries a signature non-text object… A page built entirely from text, hairlines
   and rectangles reads as an internal tool no matter how good the type is. This alone
   caps all three below the reference set."
2. **A colour system.** One accent plus greyscale. "Without it, every container has to be
   drawn with a 1px outline, which is why all three look like wireframes on close
   inspection."
3. **Vertical composition.** "That is one template, three skins."
4. **Finish at the edges.** Dead space below every interior table, uniform weight on the
   footer line, five identical badges, a disabled button standing in for a boundary.
5. **The wordmark.** "The biggest typographic liability and it's shared… 2003 sci-fi, and
   it fights every direction it's placed in. No first-tier reference in your list has a
   wordmark like that."

That last one is a FOURTH independent judge on the wordmark under the owner's new
ruling — and the first to object on aesthetic rather than legibility grounds. Recorded,
not acted on: the ruling is the owner's.

## Reader two

**C 61 · B 52 · A 38. "Ambition. Not close."**

Both readers put C first within a point of each other (62 / 61) having never seen each
other's answers. They disagree about second place — reader one had A, reader two says
"throw away A entirely; nothing on it is worth porting" — which is a real disagreement
about whether anonymity or genre-costume is the worse failure, and not one I should
resolve by averaging.

### The squint test, which reader one did not run

> "At squint distance A and C are 'big text left, small text right', and B is 'big text
> left, small text right, but dark'. That is the layout, not a design."

C is "the only one of the three with a single dominant focal point and a real second
movement" — the italic green phrase inside the headline being "a genuine second-order
attractor WITHIN the first focal point".

A: "two focal points fighting, with a big dead zone between them."

### The finding, verbatim

> "Refinement is the right diagnosis when a page has a strong idea executed loosely. Here
> the honest finding is: all three are mediocre, and C is mediocre with one good sentence
> in it. You can fix C's header, tighten its rules, fill its empty lower half, and you
> will land at maybe 72 — a competent B2B site. You will not land at Linear or Stripe,
> because none of these three has an idea that survives a screenshot at 20% zoom."

> "What the reference set actually does that none of these attempt: THE PRODUCT'S CORE
> MECHANISM IS THE VISUAL. Stripe draws the money moving. Linear's hero is the app
> rendered at an angle with real motion. Vercel puts the deploy graph on the page. The
> hero is not a description of the product beside a stat box — it IS the product,
> performing."

### The brief, which is the most valuable thing either reader produced

> "This product's whole claim is *thirteen sources were asked, two answered, forty-seven
> candidates came back, one was chosen, and every one of those numbers expires in nine
> minutes.* That is a NARROWING and it is DECAYING, and both are inherently visual."

1. **Make the hero the funnel itself** — not four numerals under rules, "an actual
   full-bleed diagram that narrows left to right, 13 source marks collapsing to 2 quoting
   venues collapsing to 47 candidate rows collapsing to 1 placement, with the losers
   greyed and labelled with their refusal reason. That single graphic is the entire
   product, is impossible to fake, and is unlike any competitor's page."
2. **Make freshness move.** "Every price on these screens carries an expiry. Nothing on
   the page expires… one live element converts 'we claim these are live' into proof, and
   it is the cheapest credibility on offer."
3. **Let the price be the page.** "$0.0136 at 24pt inside a bordered box is a stat. At
   200pt as the hero itself… it is a position."
4. **Kill the two-column hero on all three.** "It is the reason every one of these has
   competing focal points."
5. **Fill the page.** 30–45% dead vertical space below the fold on all nine screens.

Keep from C: the serif, the warm ground, the ruled open table. Keep from B: the dark
surface for the app-like screens.

### The freshness ring, flagged by three readers now

Reader two: "It carries no readable value and reads as a loading indicator that never
resolved — on all nine screens. Kill it or make it a proper depleting bar."
Reader one on the same element, and the contact-sheet reader before both of them: "a
circular arrow glyph… I read them as spinners first and had to correct myself."

Three independent readers have now mistaken the product's freshness dial for a loading
spinner. It is on the shipped surface, not only in these directions.

---

## Where this leaves the round

C wins on both cards. Neither reader believes any of the three clears the reference bar,
and reader two puts a refined C at ~72. Per ioi-c0's standing rule that two rounds
failing the bar is reported as a cap rather than iterated: this is round one, the gap is
diagnosed as **ambition, not refinement**, and the brief for round two is concrete and
came from the readers rather than from me.
